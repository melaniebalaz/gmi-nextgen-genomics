Historic Cases

A dedicated overview of selected case studies connected to forensic genetics and genomics work. Titles link to the corresponding paper where a publication link is already available.

Kaspar Hauser

For nearly two centuries, speculation has surrounded Kaspar Hauser’s possible aristocratic origin and alleged connection to the House of Baden. Combining historical inquiry with modern DNA sequencing, this study revisits one of Europe’s most enduring identity mysteries and provides strong genetic evidence against the long-standing “Prince theory.”

Schinderhannes

For two centuries, two skeletons displayed at Heidelberg University were believed to belong to the notorious criminals Schinderhannes and Hölzerlips. Combining provenance research with isotope analysis, anthropology, genealogy, and DNA testing, this interdisciplinary study re-examines their authenticity and provides compelling new evidence for the identification of Schinderhannes’ remains.

Birgitte Tengs

A landmark Norwegian murder cold case was analyzed with most advanced DNA technology. This paper examines the forensic methods, trial decisions, and eventual appeal in the Birgitte Tengs case, highlighting the risks of confirmation bias, the importance of activity-level interpretation, and the need for stronger scientific frameworks to support fairer justice outcomes.
